How I Automated Our Inventory Audits in My First 3 Months of Apprenticeship

automation snipe-it python

Disclaimer: As this application was developed for internal use at my company, I cannot share the source code or screenshots of the live interface to protect sensitive data. This article focuses on the architecture, logic, and problem-solving approach I used to build the solution.

I started my apprenticeship as an IT Support Engineer four months ago. Like many people entering the field, I came in with a head full of theory—OSI models, networking basics, and coding syntax—but very little "production" experience.

A few weeks in, I sat down with my manager to discuss what kind of projects I could handle beyond standard helpdesk tickets. He pointed out a glaring inefficiency in our workflow.

We had QR codes on every company asset. We had a barcode scanner. We had a database (Snipe-IT). But the process of verifying them was painful. We were exporting CSVs from the database, typing serial numbers into spreadsheets, and spending hours writing Excel formulas to cross-reference the two.

He said:

"I'd love to scan assets put that csv somewhere push a button and the work is done."

Here is how I took that idea and built SnipeSync.

The Problem: The "Spreadsheet Shuffle"

The issue wasn't the tools; it was the lack of connection between them.

Every time we did an audit, we had to:

  1. Export the entire asset list from Snipe-IT.
  2. Physically type the serial number of laptops and monitors into a CSV.
  3. Manually compare the lists in Excel to find discrepancies.

It was slow, boring, and because inventory changes often, the data was very susceptible to human errors caused by the workflow's friction points.

My Solution: The Direct API Approach

I realized that using Excel as the middleman was the bottleneck. I decided to write a script that connects directly into our IT assets database through the API to act as a bridge between the physical world and our digital records.

I wrapped this script into a local web application (SnipeSync) to make it user-friendly for the rest of the team.

1. Architecture: Security First

Since I'm new to the team, I didn't want to be the guy who accidentally leaked data.

I designed the app to run 100% locally:

  • It doesn't use a third-party cloud
  • It stores API keys in an encrypted local users.json database
  • It communicates directly with our internal instance via HTTPS

2. The Logic: Automating the Thinking

My manager asked for a "one-click" solution. To do that, the app needed to understand context. I built a "Scope" feature where the user selects their location (e.g., "France, US, UK") and status (e.g., "Stock, Awaiting Broker, Deployed").

When you upload the scanner data, the app doesn't just list items; it runs a 3-way logic check against the live API:

  • ❌ The Missing: The API says we have 10 items here, but you only scanned 8. (Potential loss)
  • ⚠️ The Unexpected: You scanned an item here, but they don't match your filters. (Data drift)
  • ✅ The Matched: The item is physically present and digitally correct

3. Closing the Loop

Identifying the problem is only half the job. I built a "Bulk Action" feature to fix the data instantly.

Instead of manually updating records one by one, the app allows us to:

  • Bulk Audit: Timestamp all matched assets as "Audited Today" in the database
  • Bulk Move: Instantly update the location of "Unexpected" items to match reality

The Result

What started as a conversation about "making use of QR codes" turned into a fully functional internal tool.

We moved from a manual, multi-hour Excel process to a streamlined workflow:

  1. Scan the room
  2. Drag the CSV into SnipeSync
  3. Click "Run Audit"

This project taught me the difference between doing a script and automating a business problem. Theory is great, but there is nothing quite like seeing a script you wrote save your team hours of work.
